    03

    One chain, three domains

    From a disaster alert, to the Indian satellites that can help, to whether those satellites are safe, to a message on a phone with no network.

    1. Detect. SACHET, NCS, GDACS, USGS and NASA EONET feeds arrive in Disaster Watch within minutes of issue.
    2. Assess. The Safety Check combines terrain, 40 years of river discharge, rainfall and seismic history into a rating for any place; Sentinel-1 radar maps flooding through cloud.
    3. Protect. Fleet readiness lists the Indian satellites passing over the disaster in the next 24 hours and flags any with a close approach; Orbital Safety screens them against 28,000 tracked objects.
    4. Reach. NavIC Link packs an alert into a short satellite message for receivers where towers are down.
    04

    What is real, what is illustrative

    So judges and users know exactly how far to trust each number.

    Real, live data

    Alerts (NDMA SACHET, NCS, GDACS, USGS, NASA), river discharge (GloFAS), rain forecasts, terrain (Copernicus DEM), satellite orbits (CelesTrak), close approaches (SOCRATES), Sentinel-1 radar.

    Screening estimates

    Public orbit data is accurate to about 1 km, so close approaches are an early-warning screen, not a collision call. Collision chances are illustrative. The safety rating is not an official hazard assessment.

    Illustrative

    The NavIC emergency-message frame is our own demonstration design, not the official NavIC format. Satellite status comes from the public catalogue, not official ISRO status.
